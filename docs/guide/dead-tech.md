# 📻 Dead tech, brought back

Long before fast internet, people chatted across the world over phone lines, radio and wire, and it worked. Those ideas were made for lines that drop, which is exactly what Lantern is for. We're developers with a soft spot for old tech, so we had fun bringing a few back.

## Bulletin-board mail (FidoNet), in Chat

Before group chats, bulletin boards traded messages over phone lines. A computer would call, swap what was new, and hang up, and the messages still got everywhere. Chat works the same way: your phone makes a quick "mail run" and catches up, so it never needs a steady line.

Chat rooms are called echoes, like `EMERGENCY`, just as they were on FidoNet. Turn on **Headers** in Chat to see each message the way a bulletin board showed it, down to the tear line and the origin line.

*What we changed:* the mail runs go over the internet, not a phone line.

## Shortwave radio and radio teletype, in Radio

Radio looks and works like a little shortwave set: nine channels, a tuning dial, a volume knob and a soft hiss. Your phone reads each message aloud, or switch to **Tones** to hear it the way radio teletype sent text over the air.

*What we changed:* it's a replica. The message travels as text and turns into sound on your phone. The dial shows channel numbers, never real frequencies.

## Morse code, in Tools and Access

The oldest trick in the book still works. SOS goes out in dots and dashes: flashed with the torch or the screen, played as a tone, or buzzed silently when you need to stay quiet.

## WHOIS, in the link check

Our favourite. To tell how old a website is, the link check asks WHOIS, a lookup from 1982, and shows the creation date straight from the record. A site that's only days old and asking for money is worth a second look.

*Good to know:* ICANN retired WHOIS for generic domain endings like .com in January 2025, but plenty of registries still answer. When none does, the result says the domain's age wasn't checked.
